Building High-Intent Call Campaigns That Convert

Call-channel benchmarks show why phone intent outperforms passive lead forms—and how to prove a qualified-call model before you scale spend.

In high-consideration markets, the conversion event that matters is often a live conversation. Large call datasets show that answered digital calls become leads—and convert—at rates form fills rarely match.

Key takeaways

  • Across large call datasets, roughly two in five answered marketing calls qualify as leads, and a large share of those leads convert on the call.
  • Phone intent is typically much stronger than form-submit intent, so optimizing only for clicks understates true campaign ROI.
  • A proof-of-concept call campaign should validate qualified-call rate and close rate before media volume is scaled.

What call-performance science shows

Digital marketing reporting often stops at click or form conversion. That measurement model undercounts demand in categories where buyers prefer to speak with a person—healthcare coverage, insurance offers, and many professional services.

Lead and conversion rates from live calls

Invoca’s 2026 Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report, based on more than 70 million phone calls, found that 38% of answered digital-marketing calls were leads and 42% of those leads converted during the call. Earlier Invoca analyses of tens of millions of calls reported similar healthcare conversion strength (around 40%).

Source: Invoca Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report (2026) and related Invoca call-analytics research.

Calls vs. forms

Industry syntheses comparing channel outcomes commonly find phone leads converting at materially higher rates than web-form leads. Classic B2B analyses have also reported inbound calls converting on the order of 10–15× more often than many inbound web leads.

Sources: Invoca benchmarks; Conversion Sciences summary of BIA/Kelsey and call-vs-web lead conversion research.

Proof of concept: qualify before you scale

A proof-of-concept call campaign is not “turn on ads and count phone rings.” It is a controlled test of whether media, creative, and screening produce sales-ready conversations.

Test structure

  1. Define qualification rules first. Age, coverage type, geography, intent, and any compliance screens must be explicit before traffic starts.
  2. Run a limited budget cell. Hold creative and audience constant long enough to measure connect rate, qualified-call rate, and sales outcome.
  3. Compare against form-only control. Use the same offer and landing theme so the only major variable is conversation vs. form capture.

The campaign “works” when cost per qualified call and downstream conversion beat the form control on a revenue-adjusted basis—not when raw call volume alone rises.

Operating model for Medicare, ACA, Final Expense, and custom verticals

Vertical-specific campaigns succeed when the message sets honest expectations and the transfer path protects both compliance and close rate. Broad, under-qualified traffic may look inexpensive at the bid level while destroying sales capacity.

Measure what the evidence says matters: answered rate, qualified-lead rate, on-call conversion, and revenue influenced. Feed sales disposition data into weekly optimization so targeting improves against real outcomes.
