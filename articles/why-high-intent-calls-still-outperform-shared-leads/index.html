Why High-Intent Calls Still Outperform Shared Leads

An industry update on healthcare acquisition economics—why exclusive, conversation-based demand is outperforming shared lead volume in 2026.

Shared web leads still look inexpensive on a spreadsheet. In 2026 acquisition markets—especially Medicare and ACA—teams are finding that the cheaper record often becomes the more expensive enrollment.

Key Takeaways

  • Tighter TPMO disclosure and lead-sharing expectations have made low-intent shared inventory harder to defend on persistency.
  • Inbound calls and exclusive traffic typically convert more reliably because intent is demonstrated in conversation.
  • Buyers are shifting benchmarks from CPL to outcome metrics such as cost per qualified opportunity and longer-term persistency.

What’s Changing in Lead Economics

CMS marketing and TPMO-related updates in recent cycles tightened how leads can be marketed, disclosed, and shared. The practical effect: multi-sold comparison leads that once looked efficient now show weaker buyer quality and weaker retention profiles.

Quality Gap Is More Visible

Industry acquisition analyses for 2025–2026 emphasize that shared and aged web leads often underperform exclusive, intent-qualified inbound calls when measured on issued-policy or persistency-adjusted cost—not raw cost-per-lead.

Source: 2026 Medicare/insurance acquisition benchmark analyses comparing shared leads, exclusive leads, and live transfers.

That does not mean every call channel is automatically efficient. Qualification rules, transfer timing, and sales capacity still determine whether call volume becomes revenue.

What Performance Teams Should Measure

  1. Qualified-call rate against explicit eligibility and intent criteria.
  2. Sales disposition quality—not only connect rate.
  3. Persistency-aware CPA where chargebacks or early attrition matter.
